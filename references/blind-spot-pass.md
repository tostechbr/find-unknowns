# Blind spot pass

Use when you have a lot of unknown unknowns: new part of the codebase, unfamiliar domain, work you've never done before. You don't know what questions to ask, what good looks like, or what potholes to avoid.

Give Claude context on who you are and what you already know — that's what lets it aim the blind spot pass at the right level instead of explaining basics you already have.

**Example prompts:**

- "I'm working on [X] but I know nothing about [relevant module/system]. Do a blind spot pass to help me figure out my unknown unknowns and prompt you better."
- "I don't know what [concept] is but I need to do it. Teach me my unknown unknowns about it so I can prompt better."

Say "blind spot pass" and "unknown unknowns" literally — naming the pattern is part of what makes Claude apply it instead of just answering the surface question.
